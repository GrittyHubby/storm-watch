Outage safetyGenerators, downed lines, food
  • Generators outside only. Never run one in a house, garage, basement or shed, even with doors open. Keep it at least 20 ft from windows and doors. Carbon monoxide has no smell; headache, dizziness or nausea means get everyone outside and call 911.
  • Have a working CO alarm with fresh batteries on every sleeping floor.
  • Treat every downed line as live. Stay 35 ft away, never drive over one, and report it to your utility.
  • Never backfeed a generator into a wall outlet. It can kill a lineworker down the road; use a transfer switch.
  • Food: a closed fridge stays cold about 4 hours, a full freezer about 48. Toss perishables that sat above 40°F for more than 2 hours.
  • Medical devices: if someone depends on oxygen or other powered equipment and power will be out long, go to a shelter or hospital early, not at the last minute.

Data feeds

Radar: NEXRAD base reflectivity mosaic via Iowa Environmental Mesonet, 5-min updates. Storm paths: NEXRAD storm-cell motion vectors (SCIT) projected 60 min, plus official NWS warning motion. Projections assume straight-line motion; cells can turn, split or die. Tropical track: latest NHC forecast advisory (TCM) parsed from api.weather.gov. Alerts and forecast: api.weather.gov. Not a substitute for NWS warnings or a NOAA weather radio.
